Every DJI Drone Explained: A Complete Guide to Choosing Your Perfect Model

From the palm-sized Neo to the flagship Mavic 4 Pro: key specs, who each DJI drone suits, and the downsides to know before you buy.

At DroneZoneLB.com, we carry a wide range of DJI drones — from palm-sized beginner drones to professional dual-camera and flagship models. But with so many options available, it can be difficult to know which drone is right for you.

This guide breaks down every DJI drone in our range, including the key features, who each model is best suited for, and the potential downsides you should know before buying.

Quick Comparison Table

DJI Neo: The Beginner's Best Friend

DJI Neo · Image: DJI

The DJI Neo is DJI's simplest and most accessible camera drone. At just 135 g, it can take off and land from your palm and can be flown without a traditional remote controller.

Its built-in propeller guards and automated flight modes make it particularly appealing to beginners and casual users.

Key Features

  • Weight: 135 g
  • Max Speed: 28.8 km/h in Sport mode
  • Flight Time: Up to 18 minutes
  • Camera: 1/2-inch CMOS, 12 MP
  • Video: Up to 4K/30fps
  • Gimbal: Single-axis mechanical
  • Transmission: O4
  • Internal Storage: 22 GB
  • Obstacle Avoidance: No
  • Wind Resistance: Level 4
  • Palm Takeoff and Landing: Yes
  • QuickShots: Yes
  • Subject Tracking: Yes
  • MicroSD: No

Who it's for: Total beginners, casual users and creators who want an extremely simple drone that can take off from their hand.

Potential Downsides to Consider

  • Wind sensitivity — the lightweight body can struggle in gusty conditions.
  • Short battery life — real-world flight time can be considerably lower than the 18-minute maximum.
  • No microSD slot — footage is stored in the 22 GB internal memory.
  • No conventional obstacle avoidance.
  • Noisy compared with traditional Mini drones.
  • Limited range with phone control compared with using a dedicated DJI remote controller.

DJI Neo 2: The Improved Selfie Drone

DJI Neo 2 · Image: DJI

The DJI Neo 2 takes the original Neo concept and adds significant upgrades while remaining extremely compact.

It is faster, has more internal storage, improved stabilization, better tracking and omnidirectional obstacle sensing.

Key Features

  • Weight: 151 g
  • Max Speed: 43.2 km/h in Sport mode
  • Flight Time: Up to 19 minutes
  • Camera: 1/2-inch sensor
  • Video: Up to 4K/60fps
  • Slow Motion: Up to 4K/100fps
  • Gimbal: 2-axis mechanical
  • Transmission: O4
  • Internal Storage: 49 GB
  • Obstacle Sensing: Omnidirectional
  • Tracking: ActiveTrack
  • Gesture Control: Yes
  • Palm Takeoff and Landing: Yes

Who it's for: Beginners and creators who want a small follow-me drone with more advanced tracking and obstacle sensing.

Potential Downsides to Consider

  • Battery life is still short compared with the Mini series.
  • 1/2-inch sensor is smaller than the sensors used on the Mini 5 Pro, Lito X1 and larger Air and Mavic models.
  • Internal storage only — there is no microSD slot.
  • Cannot shoot RAW photos.

DJI Mini 2 SE & Mini 4K: Essentially the Same Drone

DJI Mini 2 SE and Mini 4K · Image: DJI

The DJI Mini 2 SE and DJI Mini 4K are essentially the same drone platform.

The Mini 4K is best thought of as the 4K version of the Mini 2 SE, rather than a completely new generation. They use the same basic aircraft, camera sensor, 3-axis gimbal, O2 transmission and battery system.

Key Features

  • Weight: 246 g
  • Max Speed: 57.6 km/h in Sport mode
  • Flight Time: Up to 31 minutes
  • Camera: 1/2.3-inch CMOS, 12 MP
  • Gimbal: 3-axis mechanical
  • Transmission: O2
  • Internal Storage: None
  • Obstacle Avoidance: No
  • MicroSD: Required
  • Mini 2 SE Video: Up to 2.7K/30fps
  • Mini 4K Video: Up to 4K/30fps
  • Mini 4K Bitrate: Up to 100 Mbps

Who it's for: Beginners looking for an affordable traditional DJI camera drone.

Potential Downsides to Consider

  • No obstacle avoidance.
  • Older O2 transmission.
  • 1/2.3-inch sensor is noticeably smaller than the sensor in the Mini 3 and newer models.
  • No internal storage.
  • The Mini 2 SE is limited to 2.7K, making the Mini 4K the better choice when the price difference is small.
  • Both models lack the advanced tracking and sensing available on newer DJI drones.
  • Limited range in difficult environments compared with newer O4 models.

Bottom Line

The Mini 4K is essentially a rebranded Mini 2 SE with 4K video capability. It should therefore be considered part of the same platform rather than a completely separate generation.

⚠️ Known Issue Alert — Propeller Mounting System

The Mini 5 Pro introduced a new quick-release propeller mounting system, which is easier to use but has been linked to reports of propellers detaching during flight.

Multiple users have reported sudden crashes where a propeller detached, causing the drone to lose stability and fall from the sky. In some cases, warnings such as "Motor Overloaded" or "Propulsion System Error" appeared before the crash.

DJI advises owners to inspect the propellers before every flight and make sure the propellers and motors are properly secured.

Potential Downsides to Consider

  • Reported propeller detachment incidents with the new quick-release system.
  • Repairs can be expensive because of the advanced camera, sensing and gimbal systems.
